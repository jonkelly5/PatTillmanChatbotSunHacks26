Pat Tillman’s Life and Legacy
Pat Tillman left his NFL career to serve as an Army Ranger after September 11, 2001. His life set the standard that every Tillman Scholar carries forward.

Early Life and Family
Childhood in San Jose, California
Patrick Daniel Tillman was born on November 6, 1976, in San Jose, California. His parents, Mary and Patrick, raised three boys in a close-knit family.

Pat was the eldest and showed natural leadership from an early age, pushing limits in everything he did.

Pat Tillman as a teen
Pat Tillman playing football at Leland High School
High School Football Achievements
Pat attended Leland High School in San Jose from 1990 to 1994. Coaches told him he was too small to play football. He proved them wrong by leading his team to a Central Coast Division I Football Championship. His work ethic and determination set the tone for everything that followed.

Arizona State University
Pat attended Arizona State University from 1994 to 1998 on a football scholarship for the Sun Devils. He played linebacker and made up for his size with relentless effort. Pat led ASU to the 1997 Rose Bowl after an undefeated season and earned three consecutive Pac-10 All-Academic Football Team selections.

Key Football Honors:

1997 Rose Bowl appearance
Three consecutive Pac-10 All-Academic Football Team selections
1st Team Academic All-American honor
Led ASU to an undefeated regular season in 1997

Academic Success and Honors
Pat earned a Bachelor of Science in Marketing in three and a half years. He graduated Summa Cum Laude from ASU's W.P. Carey School of Business.

His academic and athletic excellence earned him the NCAA Post-Graduate Scholarship. Pat gained admiration across campus for his dedication to both studies and sports.

Academic Awards:

NCAA Post-Graduate Scholarship
Summa Cum Laude graduation
Bachelor of Science in Marketing (3.5 years)
W.P. Carey School of Business graduate
NFL Career with the Arizona Cardinals
NFL Draft and Early Career
The Arizona Cardinals selected Pat with pick 226 of the 1998 NFL Draft. Many people doubted his ability to start on the Cardinals’ opening-day roster.

Pat proved his critics wrong by becoming the team’s starting safety. He answered skepticism with performance and dedication on the field.

NFL Records and Career Highlights
Pat broke the franchise record for tackles in 2000 with 224 total tackles. He also set a Cardinals record with 155 tackles in 1999.

Pat’s loyalty to Arizona showed when he rejected a bigger contract offer. He turned down a 3-year, $9 million deal from the St. Louis Rams to stay with the Cardinals.

NFL Career Achievements:

Draft Position: Pick 226, 1998 NFL Draft
Team: Arizona Cardinals (1998-2001)
Position: Starting safety
2000 Season Record: 224 tackles, franchise record
Career Length: 4 NFL seasons
Life Off the Field and Community Work
Pat's NFL success never changed his character or principles. He still drove his old college truck to games, had no cell phone, and avoided flashy lifestyle choices.

Beyond his simple lifestyle, Pat loved learning and reading to develop his ideas. He then debated these ideas with family, friends, and eager listeners.

Pat used his NFL off-seasons to challenge himself with marathons and half-Ironman triathlons. He also pursued a Master's degree in History from Arizona State University.

Pat's commitment to helping others extended across the Phoenix Valley. He volunteered with community organizations and read to students in local schools.

Volunteer Work:

Boys & Girls Clubs of Phoenix Valley
March of Dimes charity work
School reading programs
Student mentorship activities

Military Service After 9/11
Pat’s Response to 9/11
Pat spoke to a reporter the day after the September 11, 2001, attacks. He expressed how the attacks made him think about service and sacrifice.

“At times like this, you stop and think about just how good we have it, what kind of system we live in, and the freedoms we’re allowed. A lot of my family has gone and fought in wars, and I really haven’t done a damn thing.”
Pat Tillman
Decision to Enlist in the U.S. Army
Pat married his high school sweetheart Marie in spring 2002. He decided to enlist in the U.S. Army during their honeymoon.

Pat told the Arizona Cardinals he would put his NFL career on hold. He informed the team about his decision to serve his country.

His brother Kevin made the same choice to enlist in the U.S. Army. The two brothers committed to serving together in the military.

This announcement shocked many people and attracted national media attention. Pat refused to speak publicly about his choice despite intense media interest.

Ranger Training and Deployments
Pat joined the U.S. Army in July 2002 with a three-year commitment. The Army assigned him to the Second Battalion of the 75th Ranger Regiment at Fort Lewis, Washington.

Pat completed the demanding Ranger training program. This training prepared him for deployment to combat zones in the Middle East.

Military Service Details:

Enlistment: July 2002
Unit: 2nd Battalion, 75th Ranger Regiment
Base: Fort Lewis, Washington
Commitment: Three-year term
Branch: U.S. Army Rangers
Service in Iraq and Afghanistan
Pat served his first combat tour in Iraq during Operation Iraqi Freedom in 2003. They completed their mission and returned safely from the Iraq deployment.

He deployed again to Afghanistan for Operation Enduring Freedom in 2004. Pat’s unit came under attack while traveling through eastern Afghanistan’s canyon terrain.

Pat died in action on April 22, 2004, while providing cover for fellow soldiers. His heroic efforts helped them escape from the dangerous canyon terrain.

Military Deployment Timeline:

2003: Iraq deployment, Operation Iraqi Freedom
2004: Afghanistan deployment, Operation Enduring Freedom
April 22, 2004: Killed in action by fratricide, eastern Afghanistan

Military Honors and Recognition
Pat was posthumously awarded several honors for his service and sacrifice. He received the Purple Heart and Silver Star for his actions in Afghanistan.

Pat also received the United States Sports Academy Humanitarian Award in 2004. These awards recognized both his military service and his character.

Military Awards:

Purple Heart (posthumous)
Silver Star (posthumous)
U.S. Sports Academy Humanitarian Award (2004)
Honoring Pat’s Legacy
Pat Tillman was an NFL player who left football to serve in the Army. His death received widespread attention during the War on Terror.

His life, principles, and service represent his true legacy. Family and friends created the Pat Tillman Foundation to honor his memory.

Pat is Scholar #1. His life modeled integrity, accountability, and action. That standard lives on through more than 1,000 Tillman Scholars leading in every sector of society. Pat’s story did not end on the field. It began there.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is Pat Tillman?
Pat Tillman was an NFL safety who left his football career to serve in the U.S. Army after September 11, 2001. He played for the Arizona Cardinals and became an Army Ranger.

What NFL team did Pat Tillman play for?
Pat Tillman played for the Arizona Cardinals from 1998 to 2001. The Arizona Cardinals drafted him in the 7th round of the 1998 NFL Draft.

Why did Pat Tillman leave the NFL?
Pat Tillman left the NFL to serve his country after the September 11, 2001 attacks. He felt compelled to serve in the military like other members of his family.

How did Pat Tillman die?
Pat Tillman died in action on April 22, 2004, in eastern Afghanistan. He was killed by fratricide while providing cover for fellow soldiers.

What did Pat Tillman do in the military?
Pat Tillman served as an Army Ranger in the 75th Ranger Regiment. He completed deployments in Iraq in 2003 and Afghanistan in 2004.

What is the Pat Tillman Award?
The Pat Tillman Award honors individuals with a sports connection who show leadership, service, and selflessness. The Pat Tillman Foundation created the award in 2014 and presents it each year at the ESPY Awards.

What is the Pat Tillman Foundation?
The Pat Tillman Foundation awards academic scholarships to military service members, veterans, and their spouses. The funding covers tuition, books, and living expenses.
